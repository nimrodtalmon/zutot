Recall · Lot or proxy?

A committee by lottery, or by delegation?

Which one reproduces the everyone-votes outcome more steadily? It depends on the population.

Both mechanisms pick a committee and let it decide; we compare how close each gets to direct democracy. Five agents on a line: when the population is polarized, the lottery is steadier; when it is moderate, delegation is.

New since D5.1

Larger committees can flip the answer

Large populations of close-knit pairs

One new result: in large populations made of close-knit pairs, the lottery is more accurate for small committees and delegation for larger ones; the switch is around the square root of twice the population.

Variant 1

Delegate per topic

A citizen may trust different people on different topics. The platform groups topics into delegation areas, and citizens delegate once per area.

Variant 1

How many delegation areas?

Each extra area is another delegation decision for every citizen, but lets each area be decided by people who know it. How many areas are worth it?

Variant 1 · what we show

A few areas go a long way

Schematic · with Davide Grossi

Splitting an area never lowers the best achievable accuracy. With b areas out of m topics, at least a b/m share of the gain is guaranteed. In experiments with clustered topics, one area per cluster recovers most of the gain (85 to 98 percent). Finding the best areas is computationally hard; greedy designs come with guarantees.

Variant 2

Representatives are busy: draw a panel

Delegation saves citizens' attention, but representatives must vote on every issue, some for many citizens. So on each issue we draw a small panel of representatives; big representatives are drawn more often, and every drawn vote is scaled up to compensate for those not drawn.

Variant 2 · two knobs

Panel size, and how much delegations count

Five representatives: one holds eight citizens, four hold one each

Knob one: how many representatives are asked to vote. Knob two, the delegation strength: at one, a representative with eight delegations counts eight; at zero, everyone counts one; at one half, about 2.8.

The panel size that suffices depends only on how close the vote is and on the error we accept, not on the number of citizens or representatives.

Accuracy gains we can prove

At strength one half, in the five-representative example · with Eyal Briman

At strength one half, each citizen behind the big representative weighs about 0.35, while a small representative weighs 1. Splitting into four identities doubles combined weight. An intermediate strength can beat both extremes in accuracy, but only under narrow conditions, and by about half a percentage point in our example.
