So a median per metric is safe too?

A project worth 1 on Planet and on Energy

Ann truthfully cares only about Planet, so she wants this project's score to be 1. Medians are 0 and 0, score 0.

A project worth 1 on Planet and on Energy

Ann says Energy matters too. The Energy median becomes 1, the score becomes 1: exactly what she wanted.

Why

No sensible metric-by-metric rule is safe from this

Theorem: no rule on fractional ballots is value-blind, metric-wise separable, metric-neutral, anonymous, unanimous, and strategyproof.

Lying can pay.
How hard is it to find the lie?

Exact score reachability: given a target scorecard, can some ballot produce it? Also an auditor's and a designer's question.

Searching for the right lie

One smooth region: easy to search

Scattered islands: a hard puzzle

Mean: finding the ballot is a linear program. Median with cumulative ballots: NP-hard. (Median with fractional ballots is still easy.)

Duplicate metrics

Round 4: gas fees, and also log gas fees

Three voters, one Fees metric

The median doubled Fees

Cumulative ballots; the rest of each voter's budget is on other metrics.

Add a copy. Same opinions.

The median doubled Fees

Each voter splits the same Fees weight across the two copies. The mean is unchanged by any such split; the median can change. The copy has the same values, so a fee-heavy project gains twice.

Four levers, four ballot types

Which rule is harder to steer depends on the ballot

Exact score reachability. Easy: polynomial time. Hard: NP-hard (worst case). No effect: copying cannot change any score. Shared hardness holds already with one voter.
