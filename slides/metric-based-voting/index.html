Why

Voters care about project scores, not about weights

Losing on one metric can be bought back on another

No reasonable metric-by-metric rule is strategyproof

"Reasonable": treats voters and metrics alike, ignores the data, respects unanimity

Theorem: no rule on fractional ballots is simultaneously value-blind, metric-wise separable, metric-neutral, anonymous, unanimous, and strategyproof. Also: no value-blind rule maximizes score-distance welfare for every value matrix.

Metrics cover projects; this is an exact-cover puzzle

The hardness comes from the data, not the rule

With one voter, mean and median coincide. Reductions from Exact Cover by 3-Sets with binary values: projects = elements, metrics = triples.

Median: the ballot format decides

Fractional

each median moves within an interval: easy

Plurality

at most two metrics survive the median: easy

Cumulative

budgets of 1 force either-or choices: all hard

Fractional: achievable-median intervals and convex modification costs give LPs. Plurality: sparse median support. Cumulative: the simplex constraint couples coordinates and encodes discrete choices.

Reachable is not profitable

Typical gain of one liar shrinks as the electorate grows

gain = how much closer a lie gets the voter to their own scores (0 to 1)
